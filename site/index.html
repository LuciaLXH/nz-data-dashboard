A guide to the evidence

Three ways to explore water.

Compare reported supply across six regions, understand what water loss includes, and put river observations in context.

Before you compare

Check the scope
Water networks are grouped by operator. They do not represent every resident or supply in a region.
Check the date
Water reporting years, population years and river observation dates differ. A recent build does not make every observation recent.
Keep gaps visible
“Unavailable” means there is no comparable value. It does not mean zero water loss or zero river flow.

Compare regional measures

Regions are shaded by the selected measure; darker shading means a higher value, not greater shortage risk. Water networks are grouped by operator, which may not match regional boundaries. Circles compare each river site's observation with its own seasonal history: orange-red below the 25th percentile, amber from the 25th to 75th, green above the 75th. Reported water measures

Compare reported network inputs and matched records. Each operator grouping covers a different set of networks. Transfers between networks have not been reconciled, so supply sums may include the same water more than once. These measures cannot establish recoverable savings or investment priorities.

Reported network supply

Annual reported inputs divided by 365 days. Bars reflect both network size and reporting coverage; they are not total regional demand or unique available water.

Supply coverage

Supply per person uses the same networks for water and served population. Counts use candidate networks after identity checks; they do not measure all networks or residents in a region. Tables scroll horizontally on smaller screens.

Water loss and its reporting boundary

The rate divides total loss by total supply for the same valid networks. Records with missing loss, zero or missing supply, or loss above supply do not enter the rate. The separate reported-loss total retains valid non-negative loss values even when supply is missing or inconsistent. Water loss is estimated and is not all physical leakage. “Unavailable” does not mean zero.

Network coverage uses all candidate networks; water coverage uses all valid reported supply in the operator grouping. Download network contributions, source record references and exclusions (JSON).

Optional regional population scenarios — assumptions, not reported totals

These scenarios apply supply per served person to the entire regional population, then extend its historical five-year growth rate. They mix a 2024/25 water rate with the stated population base year, hold that rate constant and assume all residents use it. They are not official forecasts, peak demand, capacity headroom or expected savings. No regional loss volume is inferred.

Monitored flow sites

Compare the latest river flow with the same season in earlier years. Each window is the same calendar date ±7 days; it crosses New Year when necessary. February 29 uses February 28 in non-leap years. The latest observation's unfinished season is excluded.

HBRC observations may be cached; ORC records here are historical. Check the observation date, not just the build date. These descriptive percentiles are not drought thresholds or long-term climate normals. On small screens, scroll the table sideways for history and coverage.

Definitions and interpretation

All pipeline code, SQL and tests are open source: github.com/LuciaLXH/nz-data-dashboard (GitHub Actions refreshes the data; the site is rebuilt and deployed from main).

What the water measures mean
  • Network supply per served person includes household use, other uses and losses. It is not a direct measure of household consumption or efficiency.
  • Reported water loss is estimated by providers. Some records include losses on private property; others exclude them. A comparable rate needs the same reporting boundary.
  • Regional scenarios extend network rates to the regional population, including people outside reporting networks. They do not measure network totals or predict a supply gap.
  • Coverage depends on the metric: a network may report supply but omit loss. Missing values mean unknown, not zero.

Pipeline & SQL (click to expand)

Stats NZ population and selected Taumata Arowai NEPR 2024/25 network records are combined into reported comparisons and optional regional scenarios. Python prepares source records; DuckDB executes the SQL. The site reads generated JSON. Identity conflicts and missing identifiers are quarantined, consistent repeated water records count once, and source references remain available for review.

  • sql/01 — population YoY + 5-yr CAGR (Stats NZ SDMX API, CC BY 4.0)
  • sql/02 — reported supply, matched population and boundary-specific loss rates, plus separate population scenarios (NEPR 2024/25, CC BY 3.0 NZ)
  • sql/03 — river-flow calendar-season percentile and history coverage per monitored site (council Hilltop servers)
Why I don’t claim causation
  • Ecological fallacy — council-level numbers say nothing about catchment-level mechanism.
  • n = 6 — no correlation across this few units is robust; one large council can flip a sign.
  • Different network conditions — customer mix, metering, geography and reporting methods can affect comparisons. This project has not isolated their individual effects.
  • Self-reported — NEPR/NPR figures are council-reported; metering coverage varies.
  • Consents ≠ use — the consent chart shows authorised volume, not actual takes.
Flow data caveats (read before citing the circles)
  • Selected sites — the project integrates 11 sites from HBRC and ORC. This coverage does not establish that other regions have no public flow data.
  • ORC is historical — circles compare observations from the end of the project's ORC record with that site's history; check the displayed date.
  • Different questions — a river-flow percentile is not available drinking-water capacity, and the project has not linked these sites to individual supply networks.
  • Coordinates: 5 sites verified from LAWA datasets; 3 approximate (riverapp.net / OpenStreetMap); 3 have no public coordinates and appear in the table only.
